What We Are Actually Selling

RigaCap is a momentum stock-signals product built around one uncommon promise: we manage the drawdown as carefully as the return. Most momentum services sell the upside and bury the crash. We lead with the crash — because that's the reason to pay us.

Everything in this playbook flows from a single strategic idea our analyst review surfaced: the same product earns an F for a "Market Maximizer" and an A for a "Capital Preserver." The disappointed customer and the delighted customer bought the identical signals — the difference was expectation. So the marketing function's real work is not persuasion. It is filtration: setting expectations so precisely that the people who sign up are the people who stay.

Positioning canon (keep using this language)

"Behavioral capital insurance." The anti-capitulation engine. You are structurally buying a put option on your own worst instinct — the premium is paid in bull-run underperformance.

The two tiers, one engine

One regime-adaptive momentum engine, tuned to two risk appetites. We never expose the internal machinery in customer copy — only the outcome and who each tier is for.

Audience — Sort, Don't Sell

We don't have one persona. We have two buyers with opposite emotional needs, and the entire funnel's job is to route each to the tier that will delight them.

The Calm Preserver
45–65 · larger account · often near/in retirement · adviser-adjacent

Has real capital and has felt a real drawdown. Fears the crash more than they crave the melt-up. Wants to stay invested and sleep. Buys Preserver. Will happily underperform a raging bull if it means never living through −50% again. Sells them calm and control — the F1 driver's brakes, not the top speed.

The Growth-Seeker
30–50 · self-directed · higher risk tolerance · FinTwit-native

Wants return and can genuinely hold through a bad year. Systematic, skeptical of hype, respects a transparent record. Buys Maximizer. Sells them disciplined offense — momentum with a mechanical seatbelt, not a stock-tips newsletter.

The filtration test

Before publishing any asset, ask: which buyer does this attract, and does it send the wrong one away? A landing page that converts a Growth-Seeker into Preserver — or a nervous retiree into Maximizer — is a failure, even if it converts. Mis-sorted customers churn and leave bad reviews.

The adviser channel

Independent RIAs are a distinct, high-value channel (wirehouses won't approve us). Advisers fear bull-market underperformance more than crash participation — a career-risk asymmetry. So we position to them as a 10–20% sleeve of a client book, not a standalone strategy, and we lead with the behavioral edge, the drawdown record, and client-education one-pagers. Credibility (a lengthening live record) is the gating constraint — this is a multi-year build, marketed patiently.

Where they pay attention

Paid discovery traffic is predominantly mobile — every public page and ad landing surface must be verified at phone width. On-thesis intent lives in capital-preservation and "should I sell / is the market safe" queries; that's where the Preserver story lands hardest.

The Social Engine Channel

Our content engine turns real walk-forward trades into social proof, on a reliable cadence, with a human approving every post. It is a channel, not a gimmick — its whole value is that the proof is real and the tone is honest.

Guardrails — brand safety is the product

  • Human in the loop: every post and reply goes through admin approval before it publishes.
  • No financial advice, ever. Signals-only framing in every post.
  • Result + thesis, never mechanics. Posts never leak internal strategy names or the recipe.
  • Honest by default: we don't cherry-pick only wins; the drawdown story is a feature, not a thing to hide.
  • Anti-spam: dedup so we never repeat the same account/symbol, rate-limited scans, no "Great post!" openers, no hashtag spam.
Highest-leverage move

The reply, not the broadcast. When a large account discusses a name our record already traded, a contextual, proof-first reply puts our real result into a conversation that's already happening — their audience, their context, our track record. Approval-gated, deep-link composed where the platform tier requires it.

Paid Acquisition & The Funnel

Paid spend amplifies the same filtration logic: proof-first creative, on-thesis keywords, and a landing experience that sorts the visitor before it asks for a card.

Creative angles that sort correctly

The drawdown story (Preserver)

"+0.1% in 2008 while the market lost 37.7%. A −12.9% worst drawdown over five years — the index saw −25.4%." Pulls the calm buyer.

Disciplined offense (Maximizer)

"31.4% a year over five years — with a mechanical brake for the bad ones." Pulls the growth buyer who can hold a tail.

Proof-first

"Our record flagged it before it ran. Here's the walk-forward track record." Links to the public track-record page.

Anti-hype

"Not another stock-tips newsletter. Momentum, built around the drawdown." Differentiates from the noise, repels the lottery-ticket buyer.

On-thesis keywords & negatives

Bid into capital-preservation and "is it time to sell / is the market safe" intent — the Preserver buyer's exact search. Guard the on-thesis queries: negative-match the junk, but don't over-negate — e.g. block "most volatile stocks" without killing "is the market volatile now," which is on-thesis anxiety.

Measurement caution

Analytics is consent-gated, so decliners are invisible and "0 conversions" is often a measurement gap, not a dead funnel. Confirm conversion actions are actually imported (linking ≠ importing) and check whether paid clicks reach the buyer stage before concluding the creative failed. Ensure checkout reflects the current price, not a stale value.

Retention — Where Filtration Pays Off

A correctly-sorted subscriber is a retained subscriber. Retention starts at the ad and is locked in during onboarding, before the first relative-underperformance stretch can rattle them.

Set expectations before they can break

Onboarding drip

A multi-step sequence that teaches how signals work, sets multi-year relative-underperformance expectations up front, and reinforces the tier's promise. The single highest-leverage churn prevention.

Daily signal email + dashboard parity

The email and the dashboard show the same signals, always. Inconsistency erodes trust faster than any single bad call.

"Your RigaCap Journey"

Personalized what-if from signup date vs the market — makes the value tangible and gives the subscriber something honest to share.

Real-time exit alerts

The exit discipline delivered when it fires, using the live exit rule — never a stale value the subscriber can't actually act on.

Parity is non-negotiable

Anywhere the UI, an email, or a post states an exit or a parameter, it must be the current live value. If a subscriber can't reproduce the marketed result because a surface shows a stale number, that's a retention bug and a credibility bug at once.

Referral & win-back

"Give a month, get a month" — unique codes, coupon at checkout, referrer rewarded on trial-to-paid conversion, surfaced in the welcome email and a dashboard modal. Win-back offers are serialized, never shared codes. On the roadmap: a cancel survey and usage-based churn signals to catch at-risk subscribers before they leave.
