The Sales Playbook

How to find the investor this is right for — and let go of the one it isn't. Two tiers, one job: match the person to the dial. The same engine is a great fit for one investor and a wrong fit for another — so selling it well is an act of matching, not persuading.

The one rule that governs everything
Your job is filtration, not conversion.

Do not try to close everyone. A mis-matched investor is worse than a lost one — they cancel within a quarter the moment a bull market rips and they feel they're "missing out," and they tell people it didn't work. A well-qualified investor stays for years, because you set the expectation honestly on day one. Disqualify fast, and disqualify out loud. An honest "this probably isn't for you" is the most powerful thing you can say to the right person in the room.

The honest one-liner (memorize this)

RigaCap is momentum built around the drawdown — a regime-adaptive engine that captures durable growth while cutting the depth and length of the pain, and keeps you invested when everyone else is panic-selling the bottom.

Lead with the result and the thesis, not the machinery. Two dials on one engine: Preserver protects first, Maximizer pushes for growth with the same drawdown discipline underneath. The people it fits lean in; the people it doesn't self-select out — which is exactly what you want.

The frame that lands: it's the brakes, not the horsepower

Nobody wins a Grand Prix on the straights alone. The driver who wins is the one who can brake latest and carry speed through the corner because they trust the car to hold. RigaCap is that braking and cornering system. Preserver is the car built to survive every corner on the calendar; Maximizer is the same chassis with more power — but the same brakes. Control is what lets you keep the throttle down when others lift.

The people you'll meet

Sort every prospect into one of these in the first five minutes. Three you serve; the fourth — the pure index-beater — you politely release.

01  Preserver · flagship

The Protector

Who
55–70, $250k–$2M investable, at or near retirement — "I've built it; now I can't afford to give it back." Lived through 2008 and/or 2022 and remembers the fear in their body.
Their pain
Caught between two fears: a deep drawdown erasing years of gains, and missing the recovery by sitting in cash. They no longer have the horizon to dig out of a −50% year.
Your pitch
"You don't need to beat the market — you need to grow without getting destroyed, and not panic-sell at the bottom. Over 21 years this held its worst drawdown to about −14% while the market fell −55%, and it was essentially flat in 2008 when the S&P lost nearly 38%."
Proof to use
Preserver 21yr: −13.7% max drawdown vs SPY −55%. 2008: +0.1% vs SPY −37.7%. A typical 12-month window is +10.7%, positive 77% of the time. Fully systematic — no emotional exits, ever.
Top objection
"It lags in strong bull years." → "Sometimes, and by design. That's the premium you pay for the crash protection. You're buying the brakes, not a faster car."
Disqualify if
They benchmark to the S&P weekly and get itchy about "keeping up." That itch becomes a cancellation the first hot bull quarter.
What closes
Sleep-at-night growth + honest expectations. "In a raging bull you may trail — that's the deal we're making. In the year that scares everyone, you'll be glad you were here."
02  Preserver · the channel play

The Independent RIA / Adviser

Who
Fee-only independent RIA, book of $20–200M. Independent is the word that matters — wirehouse advisers (Merrill, Morgan, etc.) can't approve outside strategies, so they aren't your buyer.
Their pain
Their career risk is asymmetric: they fear underperforming in a bull market (clients leave and blame them personally) far more than participating in a crash (everyone's down together, nobody gets fired). And they dread the 3 a.m. panic-sell call at the bottom.
Your pitch
"Position this as a 10–20% sleeve, not a core holding. It's the part of the book built to hold up when clients are scared — a behavioral tool that keeps them in their seats through the drawdown. In F1 terms, it's the braking system for the client relationship."
Proof to use
Drawdown control across two full crises (2008 flat, 2022 shallow) and fully systematic — no adviser discretion to defend to a client afterward. Down-month capture of −1.05 for Preserver vs SPY's −3.85. Methodology + risk docs for their diligence, plus client-education one-pagers.
Re-entry question
Advisers always ask "when does it get back in?" → "It's rules-based and continuous — as the regime turns constructive, exposure rebuilds automatically. There's no human deciding to buy back in, which is exactly the discretion risk you're trying to remove."
Top objection
"No long live track record." → "Correct — which is why it starts as a small sleeve, and why we're transparent about the live rollout. Start at 5%, watch it prove out, expand."
Disqualify if
Wirehouse (can't allocate), or they want to white-label a "beat-the-market" story. This is a sleeve and a behavioral tool, not a performance headline.
What closes
Land and expand. One small sleeve, transparent reporting, grow with proof. The adviser channel is a multi-year relationship, not a one-call close.

04  Release · do not sell

The Index-Beater (walk away)

Who
Benchmarks to the S&P obsessively, wants to beat it every quarter, measures success purely as return versus the index — with no weight on drawdown.
Why release
Neither dial is built to win their game. In a smooth melt-up Preserver trails on purpose, they'll feel it acutely, and they'll churn and badmouth. Every one you sign is a future refund and a bad review.
The graceful exit
"Honestly, this probably isn't the right fit — you want maximum return every year, and we're built to grow while managing the drawdown. That trade isn't for everyone, and that's okay." That line closes the right people in the room and earns respect from the wrong one.

Qualify in five minutes

Ask these early. The answers sort the prospect into a persona — and tell you when to walk.

  • "What kept you up at night in 2022?" — a real, specific answer = Preserver. A shrug = probably an index-beater.
  • "How often do you compare your portfolio to the S&P?" — weekly benchmarking = churn risk, likely disqualify.
  • "Have you ever sold near a bottom and regretted it?" — a yes is the anti-capitulation buying signal.
  • "Is your goal to get rich, or to grow without getting poor?" — the cleanest A/F sort you can ask.
  • "How big a drawdown could you sit through without selling?" — the honest number picks the tier for them.

Guardrails — sell honestly or don't sell

Always

  • Lead with the result + thesis (growth with drawdown discipline), not the methodology.
  • Disclose the bull-market lag up front — it filters and it builds trust.
  • Call every figure what it is: walk-forward, not a live record.
  • Set the multi-year expectation — this is not a quarter-to-quarter product.
  • Match the tier to the drawdown the person can actually sit through.

Never

  • Never promise beating the market or say "guaranteed" / "can't lose."
  • Never say "backtest" — it's always walk-forward. And never imply a live record we don't yet have.
  • Never lead with "survivorship-free" — it's table stakes, save it for diligence.
  • Never quote a single peak number — sell the durable edge + the drawdown discipline.
  • Never expose internal strategy mechanics or code names — sell the outcome, not the engine's guts.
  • Never sell to an index-beater just to hit a number. It costs more than it makes.
