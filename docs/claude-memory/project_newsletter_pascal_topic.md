**Pascal's Portfolio**

In the 1650s, Blaise Pascal wrote two sentences that describe your brokerage account better than most of what you'll read this week.

The first is his wager: when you can't know an outcome, you bet on the ASYMMETRY of what's at stake — a small certain cost to avoid a large ruinous one. Swap "God" for "the market" and it's the whole case for a margin of safety. Discipline's cost is finite/known (a defended book gives up a little in a roaring bull — the premium); no-discipline's cost is unbounded (the 2008, the drawdown before you needed the money, the bottom you sell). Pay the small certain premium before you know which world you're in — by the time you know, the insurance isn't for sale.

The second, harder: "All of humanity's problems stem from man's inability to sit quietly in a room alone." The surest way investors destroy returns is the compulsion to DO SOMETHING about noise — boredom/anxiety trades. The behavior gap is self-inflicted.

Both collapse into one word: discipline. RigaCap is the quiet room — trades on a rule not a mood; holds through the scary week, steps aside on a signal not a feeling.

Did the wager pay? 2008: market −38%, defended approach ~flat; over two decades of walk-forward testing it felt ~a quarter of the market's down months. The premium was real; so was the protection.

Pascal never saw a candlestick chart. The hardest, most valuable thing an investor can do is what he described: weigh the asymmetry — then sit quietly in the room.
